Epistemic Adequacy Consumption Probe

Beyond structured model access alone, does epistemic metadata change how an AI consumer answers derivation-style questions over an MBSE model? A first measured result, over the public Apollo 11 SysML v2 reconstruction.

Initial findings. This is a first, deliberately small probe: one thrust chain, one model family, one hand-authored sidecar, a single run per cell. It establishes that the effect is real and measurable, not its size in general. The full susceptibility probe, with the companion paper's three-arm design, cost-matched curation, cross-vendor replication, and a write-side gate, is still being designed. Read these numbers as a direction and a method, not a benchmark.

What this is

This is the first probe of a research programme set out in a companion paper published in the NIER track of MODELS 2026. We posed fifteen derivation-style questions about the Saturn V first-stage thrust chain to three frontier LLMs over the public Airbus Apollo 11 SysML v2 reconstruction, crossing two substrate arms with two instructions, plus a fictional out-of-distribution control vehicle. The arms were the bare model and the model plus a hand-authored epistemic-metadata sidecar. One instruction permitted reporting absence; the other pressed for a decisive answer under a simulated deadline. Across 120 judged answers, three results carry the probe.

Findings What we measured

13→60%Ungrounded

Pressure breaks grounding.

Under a governed instruction the bare model produced unauthorised content in about 13% of answers; under deadline-style pressure this rose to 60% (9 of 15). The strongest model failed most fluently, every unauthorised claim was historically plausible.

2.2%Residual

Metadata holds the line.

A hand-authored epistemic-metadata sidecar held governed-instruction failures to 1 in 45, roughly halved the pressure-induced failures (to 33%), and turned the requirement-satisfaction verdict into one all three models answered from the record.

The strongest model fails hardest

Reading the bare model, each consumer was asked the same five questions under both instructions. When reporting absence is permitted, capability protects: the strongest model was never ungrounded. When an answer is demanded, capability inverts, and the model with the most to draw on from training is the one that draws on it.

Opus answered all ten governed questions from the record and none of them unauthorised. Pressed on the five hardest, four of five went ungrounded, and every one of those claims was historically true: content recovered from training rather than derived from the substrate, which is precisely the failure a reviewer cannot catch by reading the answer.

Haiku, the weakest consumer, moved least. It had less to confabulate with.

Bare model (no sidecar), Apollo substrate. Governed n = 10 questions per model; pressed n = 5, the five hardest questions, which are a subset of the governed ten. Counts: Haiku 3/10 and 2/5, Sonnet 1/10 and 3/5, Opus 0/10 and 4/5. Single run per cell.

Is the scoring sound?

The reported verdicts come from a judge in the same model family as the consumers, which is a fair thing to distrust. Every answer was therefore re-scored by an independent judge from a different family, five times over. The check bounds inter-model judge bias. It does not establish that any LLM judge tracks ground truth; that is what the per-question keys are for.

79.7% agreement with the primary judge on the headline grounded-versus-not verdict
113/120 answers given the same verdict in every one of the five draws
5× independent re-scorings by a judge from a different model family

Recheck judge: DeepSeek-V4 Flash (served deepseek-v4-flash), run five times over all 120 answers because the judge is not bit-deterministic at temperature 0. Mean agreement is 79.7% on the grounded-versus-not binary (range 78.3–80.8%, Cohen's κ 0.46) and 73.5% on the full six-category verdict. On the Apollo governed cells that carry the substrate-arm contrast, the two judges agree on 52/60. Full distribution in Appendix A.1 of the report; replay it with harness/recheck_judge.py.

The live gap Four figures, no chain

Independent of the LLM runs, assembling the substrate exposed an epistemic-adequacy failure in the public model, present since its initial commit. Four liftoff-thrust figures coexist with nothing to reconcile them: no construct binds actualLiftoffThrust, no calculation connects engines to stage thrust, and CLR-R055 is absent from the model's own satisfy block.

Two of the four sit below the requirement minimum and one above it. The gap is not missing data. The data is present; the authorising chain over it is not.

Per-engine rated arithmetic (5 × 6770 kN) 33.85 MN
S-IC document text (~7.7 million lb) ~34.25 MN
Requirement minimum (CLR-R055) 34.5 MN
Execution-package snapshot at liftoff 35.1 MN

Verified against the pinned commit. When asked directly, the stronger models found the discrepancy from the raw numbers; the model itself never surfaces it.

Reproduce Run it yourself

The harness re-runs the probe against the Anthropic API with the same prompts, schemas, and judge rubric. Hosted models are non-deterministic and single-run per cell, so cell-level rates track the reported figures rather than match them exactly.

pip install -r requirements.txt
export ANTHROPIC_API_KEY=...
python harness/run_probe.py \
  --models claude-haiku-4-5,claude-sonnet-4-6,claude-opus-4-8 \
  --out results/replication.json
